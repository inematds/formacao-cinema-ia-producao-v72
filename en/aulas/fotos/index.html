Lesson 5 · 18 min

A film without a video generator: photos and narration with 2.5D camera

Not every film needs a video generator. Real photos or still images, a narration and one well-chosen camera move per photo already tell a story, with no credits spent and on any machine. The rehearsal clips in this course were made this way. Here you write the direction sheet and carry it out in an editor or with an agent.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1Choose one camera move per photo based on what it shows
  2. 2Measure each photo's duration by the narration
  3. 3Use the hard cut as the default and save the calm shot for the end
  4. 4Carry it out with keyframes in the editor or with the INEMA skills

Goal: direct a film from photos and narration: one move, one duration and one transition per photo, without generating video.

01 · Photos and narration become a film

Five decisions. Click on each one.

Click each block of the diagram to see what it does.

INEMA's diretor-animacao rules: never two identical movements next to each other, dramatic movement only once or twice in the film, and the last shot is the calmest.

02 · How to direct the photos, step by step

With the four images from the batch and a short narration.

1. Narration first

Write and record the narration before choosing the movements. Mark where each sentence starts: those marks are the photo durations.

If you can show it, don't narrate it: the photo of the dough makes the line "she kneads the dough" unnecessary. The narration says what the photo doesn't show.

Narration"At 4:50 a.m., the street is still asleep." · "Dona Lia is already up." · "The first batch won't wait."

2. One movement per photo

A wide landscape calls for a slow push in or a tracking shot; a detail calls for a static camera or a short push in; a reveal calls for a pull out.

Don't repeat the same movement on neighboring photos, and save dramatic movement (dolly zoom, crash zoom) for once or twice in the film. A real photo gets parallax; drawings and illustrations get no parallax, only flat movement.

3. Doing it without a terminal

In the editor, place the photo on the timeline with the duration from your sheet and create two keyframes: scale and position at the start and at the end. The editor animates between them.

Concat has keyframes with curves. Use a smooth curve (ease) at the start and at the end so the movement doesn't jerk.

4. Doing it with an agent

If you use Claude Code, you have INEMA's skills: diretor-animacao analyzes each image, proposes the shot list for you to approve, and renders through pixflow, which does the 2.5D parallax, 18 camera movements and ready-made looks.

They run on your computer with Node, FFmpeg and Chromium; without a dedicated graphics card, they render in software, more slowly. Neither of them uses video AI.

03 · Templates to copy

The direction sheet that the page checks, and a request for the AI assistant to propose movements from the narration.

direcao-fotos.txt (template)
Film: <name> (<duration> s) · narration: <audio file>
# n | photo | movement | duration | transition | narration
1 | photo: <file.jpg> | <one movement> | <n> s | <hard cut, dissolve or fade to black> | "<part of the narration>"
2 | photo: <file.jpg> | <one movement> | <n> s | <transition> | "<part of the narration>"
Request for the AI assistant (direction sheet)
You are a photo animation director. For each photo described below, propose one line in this format:
n | photo: <file> | movement | duration | transition | narration
Rules:
- one movement per photo: push in, pull out, tracking shot, tilt, orbit, static camera or handheld camera;
- at most two strong movements in a row; the last shot is the calmest;
- hard cut as the default; dissolve only for the passing of time; fade to black only at the end;
- the duration of each photo is the length of its part of the narration; the total adds up to <duration> seconds.
Photos: <list with the file name and what each one shows>
Narration: <paste the narration>

06 · Practice

The four images from the first batch are in the rehearsal clips from lesson 3; you can use your own photos.

Direction sheet · 4 to 12 photos, 30 to 60 s

20 min

Write the narration, mark the timing of each sentence, and fill in one line per photo with movement, duration and transition. Paste it into "Check your direction sheet" and download direcao-fotos.txt into the project folder.

Done when: the page answers OK: [n] fotos com movimento, transição e tempo · [s] s, with the total between 30 and 60 s, and the file is in the project folder.

Render · the sheet becomes a video

40 min

Run the sheet in the editor with keyframes (or with the diretor-animacao skill, if you use a terminal), with the narration underneath, and export as MP4 H.264. Check the file in the "Check your short film" section of lesson 8, which accepts the same 30 to 60 s as the sheet.

Done when: the "Check your short film" section of lesson 8 answers OK: [s] s · [w]×[h] · [p] for the file.
