Lesson 3 · 20 min

Editing and rhythm: cutting, linking and captioning

Editing is deciding where each shot starts and ends, how one passes into the next and what is read on screen. In this lesson you edit the four rehearsal clips from the batch in a free editor, add captions and check the exported file right here on the page.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1Order and trim the clips following the edit plan
  2. 2Use the hard cut as the default and the transition only when it tells something
  3. 3Add captions with a transcript made on your own computer
  4. 4Export in the format the page and social networks read

Goal: cut and link the clips into a 12 to 20 s cut in 16:9, with captions, and check the exported file on the page.

Six steps on the timeline. Tap each one.

Click each block of the diagram to see what it does.

02 · How to edit, step by step

With the four rehearsal clips from the batch (link in the practice) or with your own.

1. Open the editor

The example uses Concat (0.2.7 beta, Oct/2026): an open-source video editor (AGPL-3.0 license), installed on your computer, with no account, no subscription and no watermark. It runs on Windows, macOS, Linux and Android.

Since it's a beta, it still has rough edges, and the installers aren't signed: on Windows, the warning asks for "More info › Run anyway". If you already use another editor, the steps are the same.

2. Order and trim

Drag the clips into the order of your edit plan. Trim half a second at the start and end of each generated clip and watch the whole sequence at once, to feel the rhythm.

If a shot feels long, it is: cut until the action is clear and nothing more. Vary the shot size between neighbors, as the shot list asked.

3. Transitions that tell something

Hard cut by default. Short dissolve when time passes. Match on action: cut in the middle of a movement and continue the movement in the next shot.

J-cut: the sound of the next shot comes in before the image (the knocks at the door before you see the door). L-cut: the sound of the shot continues after the cut. Both connect shots without a visual effect.

In the batchThe three knocks start at the end of the oven shot (J-cut) and the cut to the door comes on the third knock.

4. Caption and export

Concat generates captions with Whisper running on your computer: download the models once in Settings › Models. Check proper names and numbers before you burn in the captions.

Export as MP4 H.264, 16:9. HEVC and AV1 save space, but not every browser opens them: to check here, prefer H.264.

06 · Optional: check in the terminal

Only for people who already use a terminal and have ffmpeg installed (the checker uses ffprobe). Run it in the course folder you downloaded from GitHub; for your own file, replace --exemplo with its name (montagem.mp4).

Terminal (in the course folder)
python3 conferir.py montagem --exemplo; echo "saída: $?"
python3 conferir.py montagem --errado; echo "saída: $?"
Example from the repository
$ python3 conferir.py montagem --exemplo; echo "saída: $?"
OK: 15,0 s · 1280×720 · 16:9
saída: 0
$ python3 conferir.py montagem --errado; echo "saída: $?"
Ainda não. O que falta:
 - O vídeo tem 8,0 s; o pedido é de 12 a 20 s.
 - O vídeo é 720×1280 (9:16); o pedido é 16:9.
 - O vídeo tem 720 px de largura; o mínimo é 1280 px.
saída: 1

07 · Practice

Free rehearsal: four clips from the first batch, 5 s each, at 1280×720 with ambient sound, made from still images. Download: shot 1 · shot 2 · shot 3 · shot 4.

Assembly · the four clips in 12 to 20 s

30 min

Assemble the four rehearsal clips (or your own) in 16:9, with hard cuts and at least one J-cut or L-cut, and export as MP4 H.264. Choose the file in "Check your assembly" and save it in the project folder as montagem.mp4.

Done when: the page answers OK: [s] s · [w]×[h] · 16:9, with a duration between 12 and 20 s and a width of at least 1280 px.

Subtitles · burned in and proofread

20 min

Write one sentence of narration for the assembly, record it in your own voice, and generate the subtitles in the editor. Proofread them and export again with the subtitles burned in; in the final film (lesson 8), the subtitles go in after the color.

Done when: the page keeps answering OK: … for the new file and, when you play the video, the subtitles show on screen with the words you recorded.
