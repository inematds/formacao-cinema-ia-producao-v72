Lesson 4 · 17 min

Color and finishing: grading, grain and film look

Shots generated on different days and in different tools arrive with different colors, and raw AI imagery has telltale marks: aggressive contrast, oversaturated color, crushed flat blacks. Color correction matches the shots and gives the film a single look. You leave with the recipe written down, and it works in any editor.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1Ask for a neutral image and add the look later, in the edit
  2. 2Match brightness and temperature between neighboring shots
  3. 3Adjust contrast, blacks and color by range
  4. 4Add grain and halation only at the end, and only where they make sense

Goal: write the film's color recipe: feeling, palette, contrast, blacks, temperature, saturation, skin and grain, the same in every shot.

01 · The order of color correction

Five steps, in this order. Click each one.

Click each block of the diagram to see what it does.

Save the look in a color file (LUT, .cube extension) or in an editor preset, and apply the same one to every shot.

02 · How to give the look, step by step

In the batch edit (lesson 3), in any editor with color adjustment.

1. Generate neutral

In the prompt, ask for the scene's light and palette (course 0, lesson 4), not the finishing. A neutral image, with neutral skin and shadows that keep detail, accepts any look later.

Raw AI imagery tends to arrive with aggressive contrast, strong red and orange color and pure black. Those three points are what color correction tackles first.

2. Match the shots

Pick the best-looking shot as your reference and match the others to it: brightness, temperature and contrast. Always compare with the neighboring shot, not the shot on its own.

In the first batch, the street is cold and the bakery is neutral. The change happens in a single cut, on purpose, not gradually shot by shot.

3. Palette by emotion

Complementary cold and warm give tension; warm, earthy and soft gives intimacy; desaturated with high contrast feels heavy; clean steel blue feels technical.

Clashing combinations, like pastel with neon, break the film. Write the palette in one line and hold every shot to the same one.

4. Finishing and aspect ratio

Grain goes last, fine or coarse depending on the feeling, never on top of grain that already came with the clip. Halation only where there is strong, visible light, like the lit shop window.

Aspect ratio communicates: 2.39:1 feels like cinema, 16:9 is TV and web, 9:16 is phone. Black bars (letterbox) on a 16:9 give a cinema look without changing the file.

03 · Templates to copy

The recipe the page checks, and a request for the AI assistant to propose a recipe from your scene.

cor.txt (template)
Feeling: <what the audience should feel>
Palette: <dominant colors and where they appear>
Contrast: <low, medium or high; how>
Blacks: <lifted or crushed; and the whites>
Temperature: <cold, neutral or warm; where does it change?>
Saturation: <low, medium or high; which color leads>
Skin: <how the skin looks>
Grain: <fine, medium or coarse; the same in every shot>
Request for the AI assistant (color recipe)
You are a film colorist. From the scene and the feeling below, propose a color recipe with exactly these 8 lines: Sensação, Paleta, Contraste, Pretos, Temperatura, Saturação, Pele, Grão.
Rules:
- one palette for the whole film;
- neutral skin, no pull toward orange;
- blacks slightly lifted and whites not blown out, unless the feeling calls for something else;
- grain applied last, the same in every shot;
- do not cite a film, director or brand as a reference.
Cena: <the scene in 3 or 4 sentences>
Sensação: <what the audience should feel>
